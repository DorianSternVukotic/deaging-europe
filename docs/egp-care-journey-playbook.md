# The Care Journey Playbook

**For the practitioners of EGP Aesthetics — Eli & Galina Pavlova**
*Prepared by DeAging Europe · June 2026 · Internal document — not for publication*

---

## 1. Why this exists — the mindset reframe

Hospital medicine taught you a rhythm: **chief complaint → diagnosis → fix → discharge.** It is a good rhythm — for acute care. Aesthetic medicine runs on a different clock:

| Hospital instinct | Aesthetic reality |
|---|---|
| The patient presents a problem | The client presents a **goal** |
| Success = problem resolved | Success = **years of trust** and a face that ages well |
| Discharge is the goal | Discharge doesn't exist — **the relationship is the product** |
| Episode of care | **Continuity of care** |

The core flip is one question. Not *"What is wrong?"* but *"What do you want?"*

> ⚠️ **The flaw-catalogue trap.** Clinicians trained to diagnose tend to scan a face and list everything treatable. In aesthetics this reads as criticism and damages the relationship before it begins. Respond to what the client raises; introduce other areas only when they ask "what else would you suggest?" — and even then, gently.

One sentence ties this playbook to your own brand: **"regenerative, respectful, never aggressive or trend-driven."** A client cultivated over years *is* regenerative practice — applied to the relationship, not just the skin.

A repeat client is not a sales outcome. A repeat client is a person whose skin you know, whose photographs you hold, whose reactions you've observed, and who therefore gets *safer, better* medicine each visit. Cultivating return visits is clinical quality, pursued ethically.

## 2. The consultation (≈30–60 min, four movements)

The consultation is where the journey is born. It is also the only place prescription-only treatment may be discussed — never online, never remotely.

**a) Active listening.** Open with an open question:
> *"When you look in the mirror, what would you like to feel?"*

Then reflect back what you heard, in their words. Resist treating yet.

**b) Systematic assessment.** Full-face assessment at rest **and in animation**; symmetry, volume, skin quality. Use the digital skin analysis (£50 consultation includes it; the fee redeems against treatment, so a careful decision costs the client nothing but half an hour). Photograph everything — the photos are your continuity instrument.

**c) Plain-language presentation.** Present **outcomes, not injection sites**: "softer, more rested eyes" — not "2 ml across the tear trough and mid-face." Pre-empt the standard fears before they're voiced: frozen look, bruising, downtime, "will people be able to tell?"

**d) Choice-based close.** Never a yes/no. Offer sequenced options:
> *"We could start with skin quality this season and look at the under-eyes in spring — or the reverse. Which bothers you more day to day?"*

**Never-do list:** no flaw cataloguing · no same-day pressure · no "risk-free" or "totally safe" language · no treating a request you believe is wrong for them (see §8).

## 3. The First-Year Map (care-plan template)

Every consultation ends with a **written one-page plan the client takes home.** This is the single most powerful retention instrument you have, and it is also your medico-legal treatment-plan record. The website now promises it — so every client must actually receive one.

- Worked examples for the three Signature Journeys on the website (Regenerative Year, Quiet Lift, Eye Edit) are kept at reception as templates.

## 4. Maintenance-interval cheat sheet (biology-first, ASA-safe)

The principle, in bold, on the wall: **say "here is when biology will ask for the next visit" — never "come back to buy more."**

| Treatment | Mechanism | Duration / course | Review window | The sentence to say |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Expression-line relaxation *(POM — consultation only, never in marketing)* | Blocked nerve–muscle junctions regrow via axonal sprouting | ~3–4 months | Month 3 | "Around month three the treated nerve endings will have regrown — that's the biology, so let's look at it then." |
| Sculptra (PLLA) | Stimulates your own collagen synthesis | 2–3 sessions, 4–6 wks apart; peak at 3–6 months; ~2 yrs | 12–18 months | "Your collagen keeps building for months after the last session — we'll see the real result in spring." |
| Profhilo | Fibroblast stimulation; dose 2 amplifies dose 1 | 2 sessions, 4 wks apart; lasts 6–9 months | 6–9 months | "The second session a month from now is what locks the result in." |
| Polynucleotides / skin boosters | Repair signalling, hydration | Course of 2–3; refresh ~6-monthly | 6 months | "This is a course — single sessions undersell it." |
| Microneedling / energy devices | Cumulative wound-healing collagen cascade | Series of ~3, weeks apart | After series, then 1–2×/yr | "Each session builds on the last — three is where the structure changes." |
| Facials | Skin upkeep (honestly: care, not correction) | Monthly–quarterly ritual | Rolling | "This is what keeps everything else looking its best between phases." |
| **Baseline — applies to everyone** | Collagen falls ~1–1.5%/yr from age 30; breakdown accelerates | Continuous | Annual edit | "Everything we do works against a moving baseline — that's why we re-look once a year." |

## 5. The two-week review — ward rounds, transplanted

You already believe in this; you called it a **ward round**. The 2-week review is exactly that, moved into aesthetics:

1. **Photograph** the result; compare against baseline side by side, with the client watching.
2. **Check and refine** — small corrections now prevent quiet dissatisfaction later.
3. Only **then** open the Map: *"Phase one has settled beautifully. Phase two on your map was X — still feeling right?"*

The review is a clinical touchpoint, not a courtesy call — and *never* the place for new selling beyond what the Map already contains. Its retention power comes precisely from it not being a pitch.

## 6. Rebooking language & the checkout SOP

The single highest-leverage habit: **the next appointment is secured before the client leaves**, while satisfaction is at its peak. The next appointment is usually the *review* — a clinical event — not the next sale. Target: **≥65% of clients leave with a date.**

Word-for-word options:

> "Your review is part of this treatment — shall we put it in now? I have Thursday the 14th."

> "Around month three the biology turns over — shall we protect a date now? Moving it later is easy."

> "I'd rather see you ten minutes too early than a month too late — let's pencil the review."

Rules: tie every date to the **interval, never to demand** ("we're filling up fast" is banned — urgency language is both off-brand and an ASA risk). One ask, gracefully accepted if declined: *"No problem — I'll note the window on your Map and we'll message you when it opens."* (That message is Template 4, below.)

## 7. The five-template follow-up pack

Wired into Fresha/WhatsApp, in EGP's voice. Personal beats polished — one detail from the visit ("you mentioned your daughter's June wedding") outperforms any campaign.

1. **Post-consultation (<24 h):** thank you + the First-Year Map as PDF/photo + the booking link for Phase 1. *"No rush, and no expiry — the Map is yours either way."*
2. **Post-treatment aftercare (24–48 h):** aftercare reminders + one personal line + *"see you for your review in about two weeks."*
3. **Day-7 outcome check:** *"A week in — how is it settling? Send a photo if anything feels uneven; that's exactly what the review is for."*
4. **Window-open / 8-week lapsed (biology nudge, never a discount):** *"It's been a while since your Profhilo settled — you're entering the window where the effect starts to fade (it holds 6–9 months). If you'd like the next session inside the window, here's the link."*
5. **Missed-appointment recovery (<24 h, warm):** *"We missed you today — no charge this time. Life happens. Two slots stand open next week if either suits."*

## 8. The ethical "no" — a retention strategy

The single behaviour that most distinguishes a cultivated practice from a volume clinic is **declining treatment well.** Your reviews already say "never pushy" — that reputation is an asset to defend.

Scripts:

> "I can take your money for this today, but I can't make it deliver what you've described — here's what it *cannot* do. Let me show you what would actually move that goal."

> "My honest advice is: not yet. The evidence grade on this one is still emerging — and on your skin I'd rather sequence the proven things first."

> "If you only do one thing this year, it isn't this — it's [X]. Then let's revisit."

Why this works commercially (so it survives a busy week): a client refused honestly returns and refers; a client over-treated quietly disappears. The website now grades your own flagship treatments "emerging" in public — saying no in the consultation room is the same honesty, and it is the most luxurious thing a clinic can offer.

## 9. Journey templates by treatment class

Mini-protocols to write Maps faster (full worked examples at reception):

- **Expression lines (POM):** consultation-led, always. Treat → review wk 2 → re-treat ~month 3–4 → annual edit. Never marketed; never remote.
- **Biostimulators (Sculptra):** series of 2–3 (4–6 wks apart) → settle 3–6 months (set expectations: gradual is the feature) → top-up at 12–18 months.
- **Bio-remodellers (Profhilo / Structura):** 2 sessions 4 weeks apart → review at wk 8 → maintain 6–9-monthly.
- **HA fillers:** treat → review wk 2 → annual reassessment. Safety note in every plan: *HA is reversible — dissolving is always on the menu, including work done elsewhere.*
- **Energy / microneedling:** series of 3 → after-series review → 1–2 maintenance sessions/yr.
- **Regenerative flagship (exosomes, PRP, polynucleotides):** frame honestly as emerging-evidence; short series → review at month 3 → fold into the annual edit. Under-promise here; the science is young and the clients who trust you for saying so stay longest.
- **Facials:** the rhythm-keeper. Offer as the standing ritual between phases — it keeps the relationship warm in the months when no needle is needed.

## 10. Compliance one-pager (whole team)

Retention activity must never tip into a breach. Non-negotiables:

- **Never name a POM in public** — no botulinum-toxin brand names on the website, social media, review replies, or printed marketing (Reg 284 Human Medicines Regulations 2012; CAP 12.12). Public framing is always *"consultation for lines and wrinkles."*
- **No remote prescribing, ever** (GMC 2012; NMC position from June 2025). Face-to-face assessment before any POM.
- **No before/after photos of toxin results** anywhere public — they constitute POM advertising.
- **Consent for every case photo:** signed, dated, unmanipulated, held on file before anything is published (CAP 3.45–3.50).
- **Proof for every testimonial** used in marketing; never publish a quote that names a POM — excerpt it and keep the original on file.
- **No urgency mechanics:** no countdowns, flash discounts, BOGOF, or prize draws on procedures (CAP 1.3).
- **No fear appeals;** never imply confidence requires treatment; never call a procedure risk-free.
- **Under-18s:** no cosmetic injectables (Botulinum Toxin and Cosmetic Fillers (Children) Act 2021). Teen Facial is skin health only.
- **Substantiate or shelve:** "award-winning", "1000+ treatments", "100% satisfaction" stay off all materials until documentary proof exists.

## 11. Fresha hygiene SOP

The website treats your Fresha listing as the **single source of truth** — its accuracy is now a marketing asset:

- Fix service-name typos at the source ("Strucrura" → Structura; "Befoere" → Before).
- Resolve the price divergences (cheek filler £450 site vs £490 Fresha; consultation free vs £50-redeemable) — then everything downstream auto-corrects.
- Align opening hours everywhere to one set (currently three versions exist: site footer, site metadata, Fresha).
- Retire/merge the legacy **"EGP Aesthetics at Beauty Queen"** duplicate listings.
- Capture **per-service share links** from the Fresha dashboard and send them to DeAging Europe — the website's row-level "Book" buttons currently land on the venue page.
- Configure **memberships** under Fresha paid plans so the website's "Ways to belong" section has a real destination.

## 12. The weekly metrics huddle (15 minutes, Mondays)

Five numbers, in the audit-loop format you trust from hospital practice — pointed at relationships, not episodes:

| Metric | Target | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Rebooked before leaving | ≥65% | The checkout SOP is working |
| Return within 6–8 weeks of plan date | ≥60% | Maps are realistic, follow-ups land |
| 2-week review attendance | ≥80% | The review is being framed as clinical, not optional |
| No-show rate | ≤5% | Template 5 + pre-booking discipline |
| Membership conversions | trend ↑ | Journeys are converting into belonging |

One question closes every huddle: **"Which client did we say no to this week, and how did it land?"** If the answer is "none", revisit §8.
